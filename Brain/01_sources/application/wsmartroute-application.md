Fundamentação da seleção da área científica
The WSmart Route+ project studies a new paradigm on smart waste management where it explores an Engineering Systems approach, that combines data
analytics methods with Operational Research techniques to build a tool that can support decision-making in waste management systems. This tool aims to
improve waste collection operations (by increasing resources efficiency and increasing the service level provided to the citizens) supporting the adoption of a
smart waste collection based on dynamic routes as opposed to the traditional blind collection based on static routes. This project focus, as well as
methodologies fall into to the Engineering Systems scientific area since it combines engineering with perspectives from industrial management in order to
address the design and development of complex, large-scale real systems.

Sinopse do CV
Tânia Ramos is an Associate Professor at the Engineering and Management Department at IST – University of Lisbon, and Vice-President of the Research
Unit CEGIST. She holds a BSc degree in Management (ISCTE-IUL), a MSc degree in Operations Research and Systems Engineering (IST) and a PhD in
Engineering and Management (IST). Tânia’s research focuses mainly on modelling and solving real-world problems related to logistics systems planning,
routing, sustainability, reverse logistics and waste management systems. Tânia is co-author of 1 book, 8 book chapters (6 international books), and 12
peer-review articles published in indexed international journals all in Q1 from their areas of research (4 Q1: Operations Research & Management Science,
4 Q1: Engineering Environmental and Environmental Sciences according with Clarivate Analytics Web of Science and 4 Q1: Management Science and
Operations Research according with SCImago Journal Ranking).
The impact of Tânia’s research is acknowledged by the increasing number of citations over the years (487 citations since 2012 - the year she finished her
PhD, and 107 citations in 2021), with an h-index of 9 (Scopus). Two of her most cited works are the papers entitled “Research challenges in municipal
solid waste logistics management” published in Waste Management Journal in 2016 (IF 7.145), with 137 citations, and “Planning a sustainable reverse
logistics system: Balancing costs with environmental and social concerns” published in Omega Journal in 2014 (IF 7.084), with 139 citations.
Tânia coordinated several national projects on the fields of logistics and waste management, namely she was the PI of the FCT-Funded “WSMART Route -
Waste Collection Real-Time Route Planning System” (MIT-EXPL/SUS/0132/2017) and of the ERSUC-funded project “Logistics Optimization of Selective
Waste Collection using Transfer Stations”, and the Co-PI of the Open Innovation Call by Sociedade Ponto Verde-funded project “Mobile-Pro-U: Integrated
system based on a mobile processing unit to recover packaging glass from undifferentiated urban waste”.
Most of her papers are related to this project. The most recent ones and specifically related to the smart waste management topic are:
A hybrid metaheuristic for smart waste collection problems with workload concerns (2022) 10.1016/j.cor.2021.105518
Searching for a Solution Method for the Smart Waste Collection Routing Problem (2021) 10.1007/978-3-030-85476-8_1
Dynamic Approaches to Solve the Smart Waste Collection Routing Problem (2019) 10.1007/978-3-030-10731-4_12
The smart waste collection routing problem: Alternative operational management approaches (2018) 10.1016/j.eswa.2018.03.001
Waste collection planning based on real-time information (2018) 10.1007/978-3-319-71583-4_22
Her ability to engage young researchers is also notable by the high number of thesis supervisions. Tânia supervised more than 50 Master's dissertations,
1 PhD dissertation and has 2 ongoing PhD students.

Resumo em português
O projeto WSmart Route+ estuda um novo paradigma na gestão inteligente de resíduos. A análise de dados combinada com a otimização de rotas é explorada
neste projeto para melhorar a operação de recolha de resíduos, apoiando a implementação de uma recolha inteligente baseada em rotas dinâmicas, em
oposição à tradicional recolha cega baseada em rotas estáticas. Todos os dias toneladas de resíduos são recolhidas e, para tal, milhares de quilómetros são
percorridos. Porém, existe uma elevada incerteza sobre a quantidade de resíduos nos contentores, facto que pode levar a uma operação ineficiente, na qual os
recursos são usados para recolher uma pequena quantidade de resíduos. A título de exemplo, dados recolhidos junto do nosso parceiro ERSUC, uma das
maiores empresas de recolha de resíduos recicláveis , mostram que nos municípios rurais 52% dos contentores de resíduos recicláveis foram recolhidos com um
nível de enchimento inferior a 25% e apenas 9% com um nível de enchimento superior75%. Além disso, as empresas de resíduos frequentemente recebem
reclamações relatando transbordos ou resíduos em redor dos contentores que prejudicam a saúde pública. Este é um caso frequente em municípios urbanos,
onde um grande número de transbordos é relatado. Estes dados representam um uso ineficiente de recursos nas áreas rurais e problemas de saúde pública em
áreas urbanas. Esta é uma situação comum em sistemas de gestão de resíduos. Para aumentar a eficiência e simultaneamente o nível de serviço prestado aos
cidadãos serão exploradas três grandes linhas de investigação.
A primeira foca na redução da incerteza em relação à procura (quantidade de resíduos em cada contentor). Para lidar com esse desafio, são exploradas três
formas possíveis de aquisição de dados. Uma é a instalação de sensores, onde são feitas medições precisas dos níveis de enchimento, proporcionando um
conhecimento a priori desses níveis. Mas como os sensores não são baratos e podem exigir um elevado investimento das empresas de resíduos, é explorado um
segundo caminho que se baseia nas observações dos motoristas sobre os níveis de enchimento dos contentores antes de esvaziá-los. Esses dados podem ser
usados para alimentar modelos de previsão robustos para prever os níveis de enchimento futuros. Esta abordagem tem um custo insignificante, mas é suscetível
a erros humanos e são registadas poucas observações em comparação com a instalação de sensores nos contentores. Para ultrapassar o erro humano das
observações dos motoristas, um terceiro caminho é explorado, onde uma nova abordagem de monitorização é proposta – o uso de sensores móveis pelos
motoristas para medir com precisão os níveis de enchimento antes de esvaziar os contentores. Isso implica apenas um sensor (móvel) por motorista, mas
poucas observações são registadas em comparação com o uso de sensor estático instalado nos contentores. Os modelos de previsão robustos são também
necessários nesta abordagem.
No entanto, o acesso aos dados (e tratamento) não é suficiente para aumentar a eficiência das operações nem evitar transbordos. Surge, então, a segunda linha
de investigação, em que é necessário otimizar as rotas de recolha em função desses dados. A terceira linha foca-se no desafio de identificar quais os contentores
que devem ter um sensor estático instalado. Uma rede completa sensorizada implica um elevado investimento dado os milhares de contentores que uma rede
opera, por isso este projeto explora a escolha de um número limitado de contentores para ter sensores estáticos.
Neste contexto, este projeto visa desenvolver e testar uma ferramenta para prever níveis de enchimento tendo em conta a informação dos sensores estáticos
ou móveis ou dos motoristas e definir rotas ótimas de forma dinâmica, resultando em conhecimento científico inovador e benefícios práticos para a sociedade
em termos de ferramenta utilizável.
O WSmart Route+ tem, então, os seguintes objetivos de investigação:
1. Estudar diferentes formas de adquirir e tratar dados sobre o nível de enchimento de forma a reduzir a incerteza associada;
2. Desenvolver e testar uma ferramenta de planeamento que combina métodos inovadores para lidar com a complexidade relacionada com a
definição de rotas de recolha inteligentes, considerando dados fornecidos pelos sensores (estáticos e móveis) e pelas observações visuais dos
motoristas;
3. Desenvolver modelos inovadores para decidir em quais contentores devem ser instalados sensores;
4. Desenvolver uma análise de custo-benefício comparando a situação atual com a situação futura em casos reais;
5. Desenvolver um teste-piloto em ambiente real.
Estes objetivos contribuem para aumentar a eficiência dos recursos (ODS9), reduzir o impacte ambiental da atividade de recolha de resíduos (ODS11) e diminuir
o número de transbordos, melhorando a gestão do saneamento básico (ODS6). Baseado na experiência da equipa [Ram1,Mor,Jor] estima-se que a ferramenta
irá reduzir os custos em 30% e aumentar o nível de serviço à população.
Resumo em inglês
The WSmart Route+ project studies a new paradigm on smart waste management. Data analytics combined with route optimization will be explored to improve
waste collection operation supporting the adoption of a smart waste collection based on dynamic routes as opposed to the traditional blind collection that is
based on static routes. Everyday thousands of kilometres are travelled to collect tonnes of waste with high uncertainty on the amount of waste in each bin, a fact
that can lead to inefficient collection operations where resources are used to collect o en a small amount of waste. For instance, data gathered from ERSUC, one
of the major recyclable waste collection companies operating in Portugal, showed that in rural municipalities 52% of the recyclable waste bins were collected
with a fill level lower than 25% and only 9% with a fill level greater than 75%. On the other hand, waste companies o en receive complaints reporting overflows
or waste surrounding the containers which damage public health. This is o en the case in urban municipalities, where a vast number of overflows are reported.
Such facts represent inefficient resource usage in rural areas and public health issues in urban areas, a common situation in waste management systems. To
increase efficiency and simultaneously the level of service provided to citizens three main research lines will be explored:
The first one focus on reducing the uncertainty regarding demand (waste inside each individual bin). To deal with this challenge, three possible ways of acquiring
data are explored. One is the installation of static sensors in bins, where accurate fill levels measurements are done, providing a priori knowledge of the fill
levels. But as sensors are not cheap and may require a high investment from waste companies, a second path is explored that relies on visual observations from
drivers on the bin’s fill levels before emptying them. The data collected should then be used to feed robust models for predicting fill levels. This approach has a
negligible cost but is susceptible to human error and few observations are recorded compared to sensor in bins. To overcome the human error from the visual
observation, a third path is pursued where a new monitoring approach is proposed – the use by truck collection drivers of mobile sensors to accurately measure
fill levels before emptying the bins. This only implies one (mobile) sensor per truck, but few observations are recorded compared to the use of static sensor
installed in bins. Also, robust predictive models are needed in this approach.

Revisão da literatura
Currently, most municipalities are not utilizing smart waste management systems. Instead, they usually rely on pre-planned routes travelled at regular intervals,
o en designed manually, regardless of the demand [1]. This leads to routes that are far from optimal and do not adjust to demand changes [Ram1]. Studies have
shown that as regions become more developed and consumer expenditure increases, so does waste generation [2,3,4]. Therefore, urban growth and national
development must be accompanied by upgrades in municipal waste management systems. To address this upgrade and overcome inefficiency in waste
collection, two research avenues are apparent:
1) The installation of smart sensors in bins [5,6,7] to access real-time information on bins’ fill levels that allows waste collection companies to: i) save
operational costs by reducing unnecessary trips; ii) increase the quality of service to citizens, by issuing a warning when a bin is close to overflowing. Recent
works have considered the use of sensor information to optimize collection routes [8,9,10], without exploring the optimization of the set of bins to be visited. In
this case, a pre-definition of the set of bins to visit is performed, usually based on a minimum threshold, and only bins that reach a certain fill level are visited.
We argue that a minimum fill level limit is not a good strategy to trigger a collection [Ram1] as containers with low filling levels can “be worth it” to be collected.
Moreover, all these works considered a full sensorized network, which is somehow unrealistic given that sensors are not cheap and may require a considerable
investment from municipalities, which are o en responsible for thousands of bins.
2) The development of robust and adaptive models for predicting fill levels [1, 2, 4, 11] which depends on historical data for individual bin fill levels, which
can be difficult to gather. Some companies have their driver’s recording bin fill levels every time a bin is visited. Yet, the accuracy of this information is
questionable, as it has lower precision and may be prone to individual subjectivity and human error. Moreover, if fill levels are only recorded when visited, time
interval between observations can be considerable, especially for recyclable waste.
We introduced the Smart Waste Collection Routing Problem (SWCRP) [Ram1]. SWCRP characterizes dynamic smart routes where each day only the “best” bins
to visit and the “best” visiting sequence is defined by accessing real-time information on the bins’ fill-levels through sensors and a mathematical model to
maximize the daily profit (the difference between the revenues related with the weight collected and the transportation costs) while considering the problem
constraints (vehicle and bins capacity, etc.). The SWCRP was modeled and solved in [Ram1] as a Vehicle Routing Problem with Profits (VRPP), an approach
revealed to be myopic, that highlighted the need for a larger planning period [Mor].
We then approached the SWCRP problem as an Inventory Routing Problem (IRP) [Mor], where profit was maximized for a planning horizon of 10 days, instead of
for a single day (as in VRPP). Our IRP model considers waste bins’ future fill levels in the whole planning horizon. As such, average rates from historical data
through simple statistics were used. By using small-size instances, we concluded that IRP models enable better results than the VRPP model, and, therefore, we
will explore IRP models in this research project. This is also corroborated by [12].

much to deliver/collect; and how to combine clients in a route [14]. When considering demand information, [15] classified the IRPs into three groups:

represents tonnes of waste that need to be collected everyday through thousands of kilometers travelled and emissions of Greenhouse Gases. The high

WSmart Route+ explores three main research streams:
1. The identification of the more adequate form of acquiring data from waste bins considering accuracy versus investment, and how to make
predictions based on these data;
2. The usage of collected data as input to optimization models that will allow the definition of optimised operational approaches to collect waste
while reducing costs and guaranteeing the communities service levels;
3. Which bins need to have more continuous monitoring to assure higher benefit/cost value.
To identify the more adequate form of acquiring data from waste bins considering accuracy versus investment, and how to make predictions based on these
data, we will study three different monitoring waste bins methods to reduce the uncertainty associated with their fill levels: i) Static sensors installed inside
waste bins, that allow the collection of more and accurate data but imply a high investment; ii) visual observations from the truck collection drivers which
are less frequent, with lower accuracy, but have a negligible cost; iii) Mobile sensors that represent an innovative solution (to the best of our knowledge) that
balances the advantages and disadvantages of the other two, giving the accuracy and precision of sensor observations at a fraction of the cost. Regardless of the
source data, machine learning models will be used to properly treat data and make predictions.
But, collecting and treating data is not enough to increase efficiency and service level in waste management systems. It is necessary to plan the collection
operation taken into account these data. Some IT companies are already exploring the use of static sensors coupled with tools to define routes, a fact revealing
the relevance and importance of this problem. However, they typically use algorithms to first select a set of bins to visit based on the current or estimated fill-
levels, and then run a Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP) or VRP algorithm to define the collection routes. Yet, several important aspects are missing in this
approach, as knowing the waste amount at each bin does not necessarily mean that only full containers should be collected on a specific day, as maybe
considering small additional distances may reveal more profitable situations (e.g. collect not full containers located nearby a full container); the service level
provided to the population must be considered, and defining the more “profitable” route for a day does not mean maximizing profit for a time horizon (as a
decision of today o en has repercussions on the following days). Regarding the visual observations, an ongoing practice at waste management companies, i.e.,
they ask the drivers to record the fill-levels before emptying a bin (typically, using a 25%-level scale). However, that information is not currently being used by
the companies to optimize the collection operation. This is something that we will explore too.
Finally, for the development of smart cities, with effective and low-cost waste management services, it is important to understand - given the large number of
bins managed by waste management companies - which bins should have a more continuous monitoring to assure higher benefit/cost value. However, choosing
which bins should be sensorized through static sensors to minimize uncertainty or provide the most relevant information is not straightforward and it is seldom
studied in the literature. We will contribute to fill this gap.

the drivers)?

deal with the uncertainty when planning for the days ahead?

5. Benefits: What are the benefits of using a system with the previous features when compared to the current situation?

Task 1 aims to evaluate different data acquisition methods and treat waste bin fill-level data to reduce associated uncertainty.
In this set, three sources of data are considered: 1) drivers records (from the two engaged companies – ERSUC and Valorsul); 2) static sensors readings
(from Valorsul), and 3) mobile sensors readings. Regarding drivers’ records, both companies have already these data and will make them available for
this project. Regarding static sensors readings, the sensors used are volumetric sensors developed by our partner Evox Technologies, that uses
ultrasonic technology and encompass a communication unit and a battery. They measure, communicate and detect events related with the waste
container's volume. Since 650 static sensors were installed since 2020 at Valorsul, there is also a huge amount of data available. Regarding the new
monitoring method – mobile sensors, no available data exist (since it is new and aimed to be studied further in this project). To collect these data, an
experiment will be conducted for 6 months, where a set of drivers will be involved and they will use the mobile sensors every time they visit a bin,
besides doing the visual observation. For this experiment, the sensors from Evox will be used, but in a mobile version, i.e., without being fixed/installed.
We will use the same bins that have already static sensors installed (from Valorsul), and thus we will be able to have different data (static sensor
readings, mobile sensor readings and visual observations) for the same waste bins, and for the same period of time (6 months). This will allow to
compare the three data sets, using statistical methods, and able to draw some conclusions.
Besides the comparison between the three data sets, we will perform predictions based on each one of them. For that, data analytics techniques will be
applied, ranging from simple temporal series modelling [19], to more complex methods that can be used when more information is available [20].
Models based on LSTM neural networks [Cos] can exploit information about each location, e.g. type of neighbourhood, commercial or residential, day of
the week, holidays vs. school days, and others. Then, predictive models will be adjusted to estimate future fill levels for each bin. To validate the
predictive models, each source set (drivers records, static sensors readings and mobile sensors readings) will be split into a training set and a testing set,
allowing the predictive models to be trained with the data from the training set, and applied to give estimates for the testing set. The ultimate goal of
this task is to compare how good are the estimates done by each source of data and conclude on the value of information given (e.g., waste bin visits
that could be avoided, overflows that could be avoided, etc.)., when proper data analytics and predictive methods are applied.
Task 1 will be mainly performed by INESC-ID team, expert in machine learning techniques. EVOX team will also support this task regarding the sensor
data set.
The main deliverables of this task are:
Paper1: Comparison of three monitoring approaches: drivers records, static sensors and mobile sensors
Paper2: New predictive models to estimate waste deposition based on drivers records versus sensors readings.

Waste collection systems are complex as they involve thousands of containers. Although monitoring technologies, as volumetric sensors, allow to better
understand these systems and to gain insights from the data collected, they are still very pricey. It is then necessary to develop financially more
sustainable strategies for integrating sensors into the waste collection activities. For that, in this task, it will be analysed in which circumstances makes
sense to have a static sensor embedded in a container to measure its fill level. A set of criteria will be identified taking into account the characteristics of
the containers and their relevance for the problem (e.g. location, type of population and facilities nearby, level of predictability, etc.). A decision support
model will be proposed, and it must be such that it helps minimizing the investment costs associated with the utilization of the volumetric sensor
technology (by monitoring only a reduced number of containers) while maintaining the benefits inherent to its use. Thus, a trade-off analysis between
the cost and benefits of monitoring all the containers or only few of them will be performed in order to comprehend the feasibility of the proposed
decision support model. The model will select from a full set of containers which of those containers must have their fill level measured in real time
through the use of volumetric sensors. Two strategies are foreseen for the algorithms to be developed: 1) Two-step approach: Select Containers First,
Route Second; 2) One-step approach: Select + Route Integrated.
In the first strategy, criteria like average fill rate, variance, remoteness, etc., of each container will be considered to select containers through sampling
techniques. Then, in the second step, routes will be defined following two approaches: 1) using the predictive methods developed at Task 1 for both
sets of data (drivers records for the non-monitoring bins and sensors readings for the monitored bins), and apply the models developed at Task 2 and/or
the solution methods developed at Task 3 (depending on the size of the instance) to define a smart collection route plan, which is updated whenever
needed; 2) for the non-monitored bins, define the optimal collection frequency and define regular routes for each collection frequency through classical
VRP models, and for the monitoring bins, the regular routes will be detoured to collect them when needed. In the latter option, the VRP with detours
must be studied [22] as a new option for the definition of smart collection routes when only a sample of bins are being sensorized and where the
strategy of regular routes for the non-monitored bins is adopted.
In the second strategy, the selection and routing steps will be integrated, since both decisions have impacts on each other. Building a model that
minimize the risk of an earlier collection route (leading to low efficiency) plus the risks of a latter collection route (leading to overflows), subject to a
limited budget for the sensor installation could be a way of integrating both decisions.
This task will be mainly performed by CEG-IST and INESC-ID teams.

Fundamentação
The WSmart Route+ project aligns with SDG 11 Sustainable Cities and Communities, SDG 9 Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure, and SDG 6 Clean Water and
Sanitation as it studies a new paradigm on smart waste management. Data analytics combined with route optimization are explored to improve waste collection
operation supporting the adoption of a smart collection based on dynamic routes as opposed to the traditional blind collection based on static routes. WSmart
Route+ objectives and scientific challenges are: 1) the development of a planning tool combining innovative methods to deal with the complexity related with
the definition of smart collection routes, considering data provided by sensors (static and mobile) and by driver’s visual observations; 2) the development of
innovative models to decide in which containers static sensors should be installed; 3) the development of a cost-benefit analysis comparing the as-is to the to-be
situation in real case studies; 4) the validation of the planning tool prototype with a testbed in a real environment. The ultimate goal of this research is to
increase resources efficiency and improve the service level provided to the communities (basic sanitation) in waste management systems towards a more
sustainable development.
WSmart Route+ contributes to SDG 9, namely to target 9.4, through the development of tools that will make the waste collection industry more sustainable,
increasing resource-use efficiency. By using our tools, companies will avoid deadhead situations (travelling many kilometres with no (or few) cargo) and low
vehicle usage situations, optimizing resources usage and reducing CO2 emissions. The CO2 emissions per unit of value-added indicator (9.4.1) will significantly
decrease.
Moreover, having more precise information on the amount of waste existing in each waste bin will also contribute to SDG 6, namely, to target 6.b, given that
WSmart Route+ will support and strengthen the participation of local communities (municipalities) in improving sanitation management. Waste bins will be
visited when justified in terms of hygiene and public health, i.e., waste bins cannot exceed their maximum capacity to avoid the accumulation of waste around
the containers. Thus, waste collection routes will be planned considering more "intelligent" criteria, also preventing containers from being collected when they
are already overflowing, with the harmful consequences to public health.
WSmart Route+ will contribute to SDG 11, namely target 11.6, as it pays special attention to municipal waste and its adverse environmental impacts on cities.
The urban solid waste regularly collected (indicator 11.6.1) is expected to improve, given that our planning tool will indicate the “best” collection frequency
(when to visit waste bins) taking into account both resource efficiency issues and public health issues.

Declaração de compromisso

Investigador responsável
Eu, investigador responsável da presente candidatura, declaro:
1- Ter conhecimento de todos os dados e informações constantes da presente candidatura e que me responsabilizo pelo seu conteúdo e veracidade.
2- Ter conhecimento das disposições expressas no Regulamento nº 999/2016, de 31 de outubro, que estabelece as condições de acesso e as regras de
apoio a projetos financiados exclusivamente por fundos nacionais através da Fundação para a Ciência e a Tecnologia, I.P. e do Aviso de Apresentação de
Candidaturas do Concurso.
3- Não me encontrar em situação de incumprimento injustificado dos requisitos regulamentares no que respeita à apresentação de relatórios de
execução científica de projetos concluídos em que também tenha desempenhado o papel de Investigador Responsável.
4- Comprometer-me a assumir a liderança do projeto e responsabilidades decorrentes da sua boa execução.
5- Assegurar o acesso aberto a publicações cientificas (peer-reviewed) resultantes da investigação financiada no âmbito deste Aviso para a
Apresentação de Candidaturas, de acordo com as Políticas de Acesso Aberto da Fundação para a Ciência e a Tecnologia, I.P..
6- Caso tenha identificado em candidatura ou venham a ser futuramente identificadas questões de ética relacionadas com a execução do projeto,
comprometo-me a:
a) diligenciar junto das instituições beneficiárias no projeto que, durante o período de execução do projeto, terão as permissões necessárias para
executar projeto;
b) recolher toda a documentação necessária e a disponibilizá-la à FCT;
c) cumprir e a fazer cumprir pela equipa de investigação do projeto, toda a legislação nacional e comunitária aplicável neste domínio a este projeto
de investigação.
Tomei conhecimento e concordo com os termos enunciados na declaração de compromisso
